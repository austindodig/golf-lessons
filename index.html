How it teaches

You'll learn why, not just what.

A lot of golf advice is one person's feel, and it can wreck the next player who tries it. So every lesson here starts with what the ball is doing at impact and works back to the motion that causes it. When you change a number in one of the tools, you see the result straight away, and that's how the ideas stick.

Ball Flight Lab

Dial in club speed, attack angle, path and face, then watch the flight. You'll get carry, apex, spin and curve on every shot, and you'll learn the nine ball flights by hitting them yourself.

Swing Plane Viewer

A swing you can walk around. Scrub from address to finish through all ten positions and watch shoulder turn, hip turn and wrist set update as you go, with the swing plane drawn right there as Hogan's pane of glass.

Tilt the green, pick a line and a pace, and roll it. The ball responds to gravity and green speed the way it does on the course, and the hole only takes putts that arrive at a sensible speed.

One lesson for every swing.

Go in order if you're starting out, or jump straight to whichever shot is costing you the most strokes. Your progress and quiz scores are saved in this browser.

Irons

Course management

Play the hole backwards.

Scroll to fly the hole. Good players plan it from the green back to the tee, so start at the pin, work out which side leaves an uphill putt, find the spot in the fairway that opens up that angle, and only then pick a club off the tee.

1 · PinStart here. The green runs back to front, so you want to be putting uphill from below the hole.
2 · GreenTwo bunkers sit on the right. Miss left or long and you've got a chip up the slope. Miss right and you're in the sand.
3 · ApproachFrom the left half of the fairway the whole green is in front of you and the bunkers are out of play.
4 · TeeThe fairway is widest at 230 yards, just short of the pond. A 3-wood to that spot beats a driver that brings the water into play.
